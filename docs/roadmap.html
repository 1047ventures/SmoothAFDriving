Major milestones

the story so far▶
T395Connect your reader from the Garage▶
The OBD reader could only be connected mid-drive, from a panel called “Sensors”. The Garage now has an OBD reader row — status, Connect reader / Disconnect, the adapter list, and one line of setup help — right above Car Health. Same connection as the drive screen, always in sync; connecting from the Garage never starts a drive. Wireframes audit item F2 (now closed).
T377Plug in → your car joins the Garage by itself▶
On connect the dongle reads the car’s VIN; NHTSA’s free decoder turns it into year, make, model, trim and body type. A car already in the Garage becomes the active one silently (two-car households get the right car every time). A new car is asked about on Home after the drive — Add to Garage, It’s my <car> (fills in the VIN and blanks), or Not my car. Car Health now files under the real car. Doubtful VIN reads are rejected rather than guessed. Untested on a real car yet — your next dongle connect is the first live read.
T375Car Health — your car’s trouble codes, in plain English▶
Every time the dongle connects, the app quietly reads the check-engine status, the stored codes, and the pending ones — faults the car has noticed but not yet lit the dash for. The Garage shows each in plain English with how urgent it is (stop soon / book it in / next service); the Home garage card reads Car OK, Heads-up or Check car. ~90 common codes explained; anything else gets an honest system-level read. Never interrupts a drive, and a failed read never shows as a false all-clear. Untested on a real car yet — your next dongle connect is the first live read.
T373“Are you getting better?” — answered on Home▶
The line under your name now says whether you’re improving — e.g. “Hard brakes down to 1.0 per 10 mi, from 4.0 — over your last 5 drives” (green), or what slipped plus one tip (amber). Tap it for that trend. Your newest drive’s recap carries the same line. It only compares like with like: drives scored by today’s engine, hard-event counts only since the Oct 15 motion change, and enough miles and events that a swing means something.
Q3Background GPS — denser tracks, locked-screen drives▶
With Always location, a screen-locked drive keeps recording densely instead of going sparse. Fixes the thin-GPS drives and is the keystone the whole “records itself” track and the live Dynamic Island build on. Shipped — you’ll feel it on the next drive.
It became a real native app you can ship▶
Smooth AF runs as a native iOS app via TestFlight. The part that matters: a git push builds, signs and delivers a new build by itself — no Xcode, no manual Archive. Cloud signing needed Admin (not App Manager), and a forced-newest Xcode for the iOS 26 SDK.
You play against the clock, not just a number▶
Destination Drive scores two axes at once: effective (on time) × efficient (smooth). Crawling to be smooth tanks the clock; driving like a maniac to beat it tanks the smoothness. A 3+ min gas/coffee stop pauses the clock.
Scoring that measures the ride, not just the inputs▶
Rebuilt on 3 signals the hardware genuinely resolves — Smoothness (longitudinal g + jerk), Braking, Momentum — plus a speed-difficulty multiplier and Ride Composure from the accelerometer, so rumble strips and buffeting cost you. Calibrated to 159 real drives; a perfect 100 is near-impossible.
Your history is yours, not your phone’s▶
The app used to know a handset, not a driver — a reinstall looked like a stranger. Now you sign in and every anonymous drive that phone recorded is claimed into your account and follows you anywhere.
A recap that tells you how the drive went▶
Reads the shape of the trip — a neighborhood hop and a highway run get different stories, a clean drive collapses to one line. Fits one phone screen, no scrolling.
You can feel the score while you drive▶
The screen edges light up with what the car’s doing — green smooth through amber to red harsh; top brake, bottom accel, sides turns. Glanceable at speed.
The same road, compared against itself▶
Drives group by road corridor, so the same route is one entry — groundwork for “are you getting smoother on your commute,” a KPI under Mission → better drivers.

Bugs

fixed▶
Fixed“Apple sign-in isn’t available on this device yet”▶
Reported by Zvi. The Apple button showed in the web app (browser / Add to Home Screen), where Sign in with Apple can’t work, and any Apple error — including tapping Cancel — showed the same misleading line. Now the button only appears in the iPhone app, Cancel is silent, and real failures say what happened (e.g. “needs this iPhone to be signed into an Apple ID”). Email sign-in works everywhere.
Fixed“localhost would like to use your location” on every drive▶
Recording already used native GPS, but three quick location reads (drive start, destination routing, the intro) still went through the web API — which inside the iOS app shows Safari’s per-site prompt every session. All now go through the same native location service, covered by the app’s own permission. Verified against the plugin’s iOS source that the drive’s GPS watcher is unaffected (T378).
FixedSeveral screens had lost their padding on real phones▶
A CSS typo (calc(env(…)+24px) — no spaces around the +) made whole padding rules invalid in every browser, so the Garage, the drive recap, the destination sheet, the leaderboard and the rewards top bar sat flush against the edges. Fixed everywhere; screens re-checked at two phone sizes.
FixedLive speed stutters & sticks mid-drive▶
iOS intermittently returns a null speed and the app reused the previous value, so the number froze then snapped — and since cornering-G and event detection scale by speed, the score degraded with it. Now deriveSpeed() falls back to distance-moved between fixes, with a deadband and a cap. Feeds the score, not just the display.
FixedGPS tracks were world-readable▶
The select policy was using (true) — anyone with the anon key could read every coordinate track. A security-definer function now returns only one device’s unclaimed drives; reads are owner-only. Verified from outside: anonymous select returns empty, restore still returns all 122 drives.
FixedLeaderboard wrote to a table that never existed▶
Every score upload 404’d into a silent catch for months. Standing is now derived on the server from recorded drives. The five invented competitors are gone. This is rewards & leaderboard’s foundation, already laid.
FixedFour deploy pipelines → one▶
Netlify and Vercel retired — Cloudflare Pages is the sole host. The /api/* functions, admin dashboard and signup capture all moved there. (If the old git integrations are still linked on those platforms, unlink them to stop duplicate builds.)
FixedMap attribution bled through the score breakdown▶
Leaflet’s attribution containers default to z-index:1000, outranking the breakdown sheet at 900. The map block is now its own stacking context, so every z-index Leaflet sets is contained inside it.

Tech debt

8 open▶
Pre-launchThree database functions trust a bare ID▶
Fix built and pushed; live once the SQL above is pasted. The two developer read functions are dropped (the dev pull now goes through the password-gated admin endpoint). Deleting, claiming and anonymous restore now need proof of ownership: your account when signed in, or a private key only the recording phone holds. What’s left: drives uploaded before Oct 17 carry no key, so they stay reachable by device ID until they’re claimed. The admin password check also has no lockout beyond a delay.
IntegrityScores are computed on the phone▶
The leaderboard is built server-side from saved drives, but each drive’s score is what the app uploaded. A determined user could post a score they didn’t drive. Fix: score on the server from the uploaded track.
ScoringBraking approach length doesn’t vary▶
The braking score averages approach length and deceleration, but the approach length is a fixed 30-sample window, so only deceleration actually moves the number. Needs a real approach measure (from where you came off the gas).
SmallPoints don’t follow you to a new phone yet▶
Drives restored from the cloud come back without their GPS track or smoothness, so points are estimated from the score and the on-time bonus is lost. Fix: store each drive’s points in the cloud row and read them back on restore (needs one small database column).
OptionalSUPABASE_DB_URL secret — stops the failure emails▶
The secret holds the password alone, so the auto-migrate job fails on every push that touches the database. Harmless — changes get applied by hand in the SQL editor instead. Fixing it (Session pooler connection string) just makes them apply themselves and stops the emails.
SecurityRotate the remove.bg API key▶
A key was hardcoded and shipped in every client bundle. The fallback is removed and the app prompts for your own key, but the old one should be treated as compromised and revoked at remove.bg.
Confusingsmoothafdriving.com/tracker serves a dead old tracker▶
The Lovable marketing site (DNS at IONOS) still hosts a stale tracker at /tracker — what made the app look broken. Decision: the domain stays brand/marketing; this page should become a “Download the app” link.
PolishUX-audit leftovers▶
Modal focus management and a couple of missing empty states (polish). The two specced-not-built items map up: first-run onboarding → the modes idea (first-timer), and vehicle fingerprinting → keyed-to-your-car (Car Health / the map features).

Mission

measured · 3 parts▶
Make people genuinely better, smoother drivers▶
US roads suck because driving is treated as a skill you got at 16 and never revisit. We want to be part of changing that — efficient, systems-thinking drivers who read the whole road. The score makes you want to be smooth; the app teaches you how. Improvement, not the number, is the product. KPIs • Improvement rate — % of returning drivers whose rolling-avg score climbs over time (per-driver verdict shipped T373; fleet-wide % still to build)
• Harsh events / mile — trending down across a driver’s own history
• Same-corridor delta — smoother on your commute than a month ago Baseline today (live /admin) • 86 avg score · 4 drivers (3 besides you, 11 drives between them) · 217 drives scored Tasks that move it • honest scoring (shipped — a fair score is the prerequisite) · corridor comparison (shipped) · rewards & leaderboard rewards (the pull to improve) · the coach, below
Modes — one app, never-driven to pro▶
It’s an education app as much as a tracker. Same engine, different voice: First Timer (pedals, signals, mirror & seat setup, CarPlay, first turns — with gentle “check your mirrors / scan who’s around / think in systems” nudges), Teen (parent-linked, logs practice hours), Smooth Operator (the everyday loop), Pro (all the data, Max-G pings, leaderboards, prizes), Road-tripper/RV. Modes subtract — a pro never sees “check your mirrors,” a first-timer never sees “Max G.” KPIs • Modes shipped — 0 of 5 today (one-size app)
• Teen + parent signups — the wedge
• First-timer walkthrough completion Tasks that move it • Q9 Goals (the first per-driver tuning) · first-run onboarding (specced, not built — see Tech debt) · the modes build itself (not yet queued)
Smooth AF driving coaches▶
The app coaches you free every drive — tips, trends, drills. When a pattern won’t budge, a real coach reading telemetry no instructor ever had (the Driving Fingerprint, harsh-moment map markers, the g-arc through each corner) sends drills tuned to your patterns. Async review → live ride-along → structured programs. Teens + parents are the wedge; it’s a coaching marketplace. KPIs • Coached drivers / sessions — 0 today
• Retention — coached vs uncoached
• Marketplace GMV — the far-horizon number Tasks that move it • the driving fingerprint in Values & Vision (the telemetry edge) · harsh-moment markers (shipped) · async-review tooling (unbuilt)
