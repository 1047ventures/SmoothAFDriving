Long-term vision

Your Car’s Concierge▶

Where the queue leads — one honest app for your whole drive, keyed to the car you’re actually in. The map gets you there, the score makes you want to be smooth, the coach makes you good at it; OBD is the platform underneath, not a feature. These are the far ends of the four tracks, and the money.

from Track 1Car Health, paid — the recurring-revenue line▶
On top of the free fault-code scan (Q5): free = scan + basic alert; paid = proactive heads-up before the light, a maintenance timeline keyed to your mileage, seasonal tips, and a tap through to a DIY video or a local mobile mechanic. This is why OBD is the platform, not just a gauge.
Track 3Be the drive layer — the map, keyed to your car▶
Unlocked once the app reliably owns the drive (Track 2):
• Vehicle-aware directions — no interstate shortcut when you’re in an RV. Road-type avoidance works on Mapbox today; true RV-grade (bridge heights, weight limits) needs a specialized API.
• Popular & scenic routes nearby — a reason to open the app when you’re not just commuting.
• Contextual services — gas, EV chargers, parking, surfaced only when they help (low tank, nearing your stop) then out of the way. EV chargers are a free API, parking has partner APIs, gas pricing is the paid one.
from MissionThe coaching marketplace▶
The far end of Mission → coaches: async review → live ride-along → structured programs, with teens + parents as the wedge. A real coach reading telemetry no instructor ever had — the Driving Fingerprint, harsh-moment markers, the g-arc through each corner.

Major milestones

the story so far▶

Just shipped, newest first — then the story so far. The exhaustive log is TASKS.md.

T365No more motion prompt — zero, ever▶
That “allow motion” alert at the start of every drive is gone for good. It came from the web motion API, which iOS re-asks every session. The app now reads motion through native Core Motion, which needs no permission and no prompt at all. Native plugin verified building on the simulator; lands on the next TestFlight build.
T363Your throttle foot is now part of the score▶
The OBD throttle channel — how you work the pedal — now feeds the smoothness score: a progressive squeeze reads smooth, stabbing the pedal costs you. Brake and steering stay out on purpose — neither is a real OBD signal, and the phone already scores their result (braking g, cornering g). GPS-only drives are unchanged.
T362Avg MPH is back · App Store location fix▶
Avg MPH returns to the live stats bar (Stops · Miles · Avg · Time), and the iOS build now carries the background-location purpose string App Store Connect flagged (ITMS-90683).
T360OBD auto-starts the drive · live gauges on screen▶
The dongle links up and the drive just starts (one-tap undo), with RPM / HP / coolant / boost on the driving screen. The first piece of Track 2 already in your hands.
It became a real native app you can ship▶
Smooth AF runs as a native iOS app via TestFlight. The part that matters: a git push builds, signs and delivers a new build by itself — no Xcode, no manual Archive. Cloud signing needed Admin (not App Manager), and a forced-newest Xcode for the iOS 26 SDK.
You play against the clock, not just a number▶
Destination Drive scores two axes at once: effective (on time) × efficient (smooth). Crawling to be smooth tanks the clock; driving like a maniac to beat it tanks the smoothness. A 3+ min gas/coffee stop pauses the clock.
Scoring that measures the ride, not just the inputs▶
Rebuilt on 3 signals the hardware genuinely resolves — Smoothness (longitudinal g + jerk), Braking, Momentum — plus a speed-difficulty multiplier and Ride Composure from the accelerometer, so rumble strips and buffeting cost you. Calibrated to 159 real drives; a perfect 100 is near-impossible.
Your history is yours, not your phone’s▶
The app used to know a handset, not a driver — a reinstall looked like a stranger. Now you sign in and every anonymous drive that phone recorded is claimed into your account and follows you anywhere.
A recap that tells you how the drive went▶
Reads the shape of the trip — a neighborhood hop and a highway run get different stories, a clean drive collapses to one line. Fits one phone screen, no scrolling.
You can feel the score while you drive▶
The screen edges light up with what the car’s doing — green smooth through amber to red harsh; top brake, bottom accel, sides turns. Glanceable at speed.
The same road, compared against itself▶
Drives group by road corridor, so the same route is one entry — groundwork for “are you getting smoother on your commute,” a KPI under Mission → better drivers.

Bugs

fixed▶

All fixed — and these are the foundations the tracks now stand on. Two matter most: the server-derived leaderboard made honest standings possible at all (Track 4), and locking down the GPS tracks is the privacy floor under any health/location data (Track 1). The live-speed fix is core-engine accuracy, same family as Road’s-fault.

FixedLive speed stutters & sticks mid-drive▶
iOS intermittently returns a null speed and the app reused the previous value, so the number froze then snapped — and since cornering-G and event detection scale by speed, the score degraded with it. Now deriveSpeed() falls back to distance-moved between fixes, with a deadband and a cap. Feeds the score, not just the display.
FixedGPS tracks were world-readable▶
The select policy was using (true) — anyone with the anon key could read every coordinate track. A security-definer function now returns only one device’s unclaimed drives; reads are owner-only. Verified from outside: anonymous select returns empty, restore still returns all 122 drives.
FixedLeaderboard wrote to a table that never existed▶
Every score upload 404’d into a silent catch for months. Standing is now derived on the server from recorded drives. The five invented competitors are gone. This is Track 4’s foundation, already laid.
FixedFour deploy pipelines → one▶
Netlify and Vercel retired — Cloudflare Pages is the sole host. The /api/* functions, admin dashboard and signup capture all moved there. (If the old git integrations are still linked on those platforms, unlink them to stop duplicate builds.)
FixedMap attribution bled through the score breakdown▶
Leaflet’s attribution containers default to z-index:1000, outranking the breakdown sheet at 900. The map block is now its own stacking context, so every z-index Leaflet sets is contained inside it.

Mission

measured · 3 pillars▶

The why, made measurable. Each pillar carries the KPIs we judge it by and the tasks that move them — most of those metrics aren’t instrumented yet, so the list doubles as what to start measuring.

Make people genuinely better, smoother drivers▶
US roads suck because driving is treated as a skill you got at 16 and never revisit. We want to be part of changing that — efficient, systems-thinking drivers who read the whole road. The score makes you want to be smooth; the app teaches you how. Improvement, not the number, is the product. KPIs • Improvement rate — % of returning drivers whose rolling-avg score climbs over time (metric to build)
• Harsh events / mile — trending down across a driver’s own history
• Same-corridor delta — smoother on your commute than a month ago Baseline today (live /admin) • 86 avg score · 2 returning drivers · 195 drives scored Tasks that move it • Q4 honest scoring (a fair score is the prerequisite) · corridor comparison (shipped) · Track 4 rewards (the pull to improve) · the coach, below
Modes — one app, never-driven to pro▶
It’s an education app as much as a tracker. Same engine, different voice: First Timer (pedals, signals, mirror & seat setup, CarPlay, first turns — with gentle “check your mirrors / scan who’s around / think in systems” nudges), Teen (parent-linked, logs practice hours), Smooth Operator (the everyday loop), Pro (all the data, Max-G pings, leaderboards, prizes), Road-tripper/RV. Modes subtract — a pro never sees “check your mirrors,” a first-timer never sees “Max G.” KPIs • Modes shipped — 0 of 5 today (one-size app)
• Teen + parent signups — the wedge
• First-timer walkthrough completion Tasks that move it • Q9 Goals (the first per-driver tuning) · first-run onboarding (specced, not built — see Tech debt) · the modes build itself (not yet queued)
Smooth AF driving coaches▶
The app coaches you free every drive — tips, trends, drills. When a pattern won’t budge, a real coach reading telemetry no instructor ever had (the Driving Fingerprint, harsh-moment map markers, the g-arc through each corner) sends drills tuned to your patterns. Async review → live ride-along → structured programs. Teens + parents are the wedge; it’s a coaching marketplace. KPIs • Coached drivers / sessions — 0 today
• Retention — coached vs uncoached
• Marketplace GMV — the far-horizon number Tasks that move it • the Driving Fingerprint (the telemetry edge) · harsh-moment markers (shipped) · async-review tooling (unbuilt)
