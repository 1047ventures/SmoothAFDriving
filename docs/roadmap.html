Waiting on you

4 things
1 · Before you drive Engine OFF — is the dongle still visible? 2 min · decides a design ▶

Plug the Veepeak in, then with the engine switched off, open any OBD scanner app (Car Scanner ELM OBD2, free) and look at its Bluetooth device list.

Tell me one thing: does the dongle still appear, yes or no?

DisappearsThe dongle powers down with the ignition — so it can be the auto-start trigger. The app can wake itself when the car starts.
Still thereIt stays live on constant 12V. Auto-start has to use motion/location instead, and the dongle is slowly draining your battery when parked.

This is the only reason I can’t just build auto-start — the answer picks the mechanism.

2 · In the car Connect the dongle and send me the readout 3 min · parked ▶

Wait for the new TestFlight build first — the OBD panel does not exist in build 106. (Two builds failed to ship earlier today; that’s fixed, see below.)

  1. Engine running, parked. Open the app → Start Drive.
  2. Tap the “⌃ Sensors” pill near the bottom.
  3. Tap Connect OBD and pick the Veepeak from the list.
  4. Blip the throttle a couple of times.

Send me the status line and the four numbers (throttle / rpm / speed / load). That tells me whether your car reports throttle plate or pedal position, which decides how it feeds the score.

Expect rough edges. No one has tested this against real hardware — the adapter’s Bluetooth identifiers aren’t standardised, so the app discovers them at runtime. If it says “That device isn’t an OBD adapter”, that is useful information, not a dead end.
3 · One secret Add the Mapbox token to GitHub so shipped builds get traffic ETAs 1 min ▶

Your token is in the local .env and working — but CI builds don’t see it, so TestFlight builds would still ship on OSRM (no traffic) until the secret exists.

Add a repo secret named VITE_MAPBOX_TOKEN at GitHub → Secrets → Actions, value your pk. token. Both build workflows already read it. A pk. token is public by design (it ships in the app either way), so this isn’t a sensitive secret — just where CI looks for it.

4 · Optional Turn on Sign in with Apple button appears by itself ▶

Already built and shipped — it checks Supabase at runtime and appears on its own the moment you enable the provider. No new app release needed.

Skippable: Apple only requires Sign in with Apple if you offer another third-party login. Email-only is App Store submittable as-is.

  1. developer.apple.com → Identifiers → your App ID → tick Sign In with Apple.
  2. Xcode → App target → Signing & Capabilities → + Capability → Sign In with Apple.
  3. Supabase → Authentication → Providers → Apple → enable, add com.smoothafdriving.app to Authorized Client IDs.
  4. Tell me and I’ll add the native plugin and ship it.

The big one

It stopped being a website and became an app you can ship

Smooth AF now runs as a real native iOS app — same code, wrapped in a native shell, installed on your phone through TestFlight and capable of going to the App Store. The part that matters day to day: a git push now builds, signs and delivers a new build by itself. No Xcode, no manual Archive, no waiting on you.

Getting here meant clearing a lot of Apple's undergrowth — an enrolled Developer account, cloud signing that needed Admin rather than App Manager, and a forced-newest Xcode once Apple started requiring the iOS 26 SDK. Xcode Cloud got abandoned along the way for GitHub Actions, which actually works.

open Xcode, archive, upload, repeat→push to main, done
The game

You play against the clock, not just a smoothness number

Destination Drive turned a logger into a game. Set a destination, get a live traffic-aware ETA, and score on two axes at once: effective (did you actually get there on time) × efficient (did you do it smoothly). Gaming it doesn't work — being smooth by crawling tanks the clock, and beating the clock by driving like a maniac tanks the smoothness.

A 3+ minute stop for gas or coffee pauses the clock, so a legitimate errand can't make you "late."

The engine

Scoring that holds up to a real drive

Every drive is graded across seven independent dimensions — peak harshness, throttle, steering, braking anticipation, corner composure, transitions, momentum — then blended into one composite. Short drives get pulled toward the mean on purpose, so a 30-second test drive can't score a perfect 100.

All of it is covered by 235 automated tests that run on every change.

Identity

Your history is yours now, not your phone's

The app used to know a handset, not a driver — a new phone or a reinstall looked like a total stranger, and 122 drives could vanish with one device. Now you sign in, and every drive that phone recorded anonymously gets claimed into your account and follows you anywhere.

a random ID per install→an account that travels
The payoff

A recap that tells you how the drive went

The post-drive screen reads the shape of the trip and says something true about it — a quick neighborhood hop and a long highway run get different stories, and a clean drive collapses to a single line instead of padding. Numbers live in their own row, and the whole thing fits one phone screen with no scrolling.

In the car

You can feel the score while you drive

The screen edges light up with what the car is actually doing — green smooth through amber to red harsh, top for braking, bottom for acceleration, sides for turns. Glanceable at speed, no reading required.

Over time

The same road, compared against itself

Drives get grouped by road corridor, so the same route is one entry no matter where you joined it — the groundwork for "are you actually getting smoother on your commute," rather than a flat list of one-off scores.

QueuedHand the destination to Apple / Google Maps blocked by background recording▶

Opening a destination in Maps is a two-line change — a maps:// or comgooglemaps:// link. That is not the hard part.

The real problem: launching Maps backgrounds Smooth AF, and iOS suspends a backgrounded web view. GPS stops, the drive stops being scored, and you arrive to find nothing recorded — which is worse than having no Maps button at all. The crash-recovery snapshot the app already takes every 30s exists precisely because backgrounding eats drives.

So the work is background location, not the link: the native shell needs the location background mode and Always permission so recording survives while you navigate in another app. Once that holds, the handoff button is trivial and so is the return trip.

A middle option worth considering: keep navigation in Maps for your eyes, and show only a next-turn hint in our HUD from the Mapbox route we already fetch — no second map to maintain, no second set of tiles to pay for.

QueuedEvery home-screen element earns its place tap for depth, or remove▶

The rule: if it is on the home screen it should open something on tap — and if there is nothing worth opening, it should not be competing for space there.

That cuts both ways, which is what makes it a useful rule. Each element gets sorted into one of three outcomes: give it a detail view (a score opens its history, a stat opens its trend), merge it into something that already has one, or cut it. Expect the audit to delete as much as it links.

The audit is done and lives here: Home Screen Audit — every element sorted by verdict, kept up to date as this changes.

Its finding, in short: applied strictly the rule produces two unfinished features and one corpse, not a list of tap targets to bolt on. The linking work is a couple of hours; the decision about the sparkline and the persona block is the actual work.

Flagged / tech debt

Fixed
GPS tracks are no longer world-readable
The select policy was using (true) — anyone holding the anon key, which ships inside the client, could read every coordinate track in the database. It stayed open because closing it appeared to cost anonymous cloud restore. That trade was false: restore never needed a blanket read, only the drives of one device id, which a security-definer function now returns — and only unclaimed ones, so a resold phone can’t pull back history since claimed. Reads are owner-only; anonymous callers have no read policy at all. Verified from outside: an anonymous select returns empty, restore still returns all 122 drives.
Fixed
The leaderboard was writing to a table that never existed
Every score upload 404’d into a silent catch, so the feature looked implemented and did nothing for months. Had the table existed it would have been worse — anyone with the anon key could have posted any score under any name. Standing is now derived on the server from recorded drives. The five invented competitors are gone too: fine as set dressing, dishonest once a ranking is attached to a discount.
Unfinished
Leaderboard "Corridors" / "Rivals" tabs
Permanent empty placeholders — the road-trends fix fills these.
Cleanup
Four deploy pipelines → one
Netlify + 2× Vercel + Cloudflare all build every push, and the domain points at none of them. Pick one owner (Cloudflare Pages) and retire the rest — this is what caused the dead-app confusion.
Fixed
Drives come back down again
The app only ever pushed, so a reinstall showed an empty history while the data sat safely in the cloud. Restore now works two ways: signed in it pulls everything the account owns across every device, and signed out it still recovers this device’s own drives. Your 122 drives / 403 mi are claimed to your account.
Confusing
smoothafdriving.com/tracker serves a dead old tracker
The Lovable marketing site (separate codebase, DNS at IONOS) still hosts a stale tracker at /tracker — which is what made the app look broken. Decision made: the domain stays brand/marketing/coaching; the app ships independently via the App Store. So this page should just become a "Download the app" link. Not blocking anything.
Security
Rotate the remove.bg API key
A key was hardcoded in the source, committed, and shipped inside every client bundle — anyone with the app could read it out and spend the quota. The fallback is removed and the app now prompts for your own key, but the old one should be treated as compromised and revoked at remove.bg.
Fixed
OpenStreetMap attribution bleeding through the score breakdown
Leaflet’s .leaflet-top/.leaflet-bottom containers default to z-index:1000, which outranked the breakdown sheet at 900 — so the attribution painted over it. An existing rule pinned .leaflet-control-container, which is not the element carrying that value. The map block is now its own stacking context, so every z-index Leaflet sets is contained inside it and cannot reach a sheet above. Containing the subtree beats pinning controls one at a time.

Modal focus management, the dead "Explore Rewards" button, a couple of missing empty states.

✓
ETAs are honest now — real traffic, no phantom padding Aug 16
Beating a 40-minute drive by 14 minutes was two bugs stacked. There was no traffic data at all — with no Mapbox token the app fell back to OSRM free-flow times (no lights, no congestion), so the "traffic-aware ETA" wasn’t actually running. And a flat 1.2 buffer padded the target another 20% on top, with the hook meant to remove it never wired in. Now Mapbox driving-traffic supplies the ETA and the buffer follows the provider: 1.2 for OSRM, 1.05 for a Mapbox time that already counts traffic. Each drive is stamped with the buffer it was scored under, so this can’t retroactively re-judge old drives.
✓
The app can read your car — OBD-II over Bluetooth Aug 16
Throttle position, RPM, true wheel speed and engine load, polled about four times a second — data the phone's own sensors cannot see. GPS speed is a lagging, smoothed derivative of position; the car knows immediately. The adapter's Bluetooth identifiers aren't standardised across ELM327 clones, so rather than hardcode a guess the app interrogates whatever you plug in and pairs the first characteristic that can notify with the first that can be written. Connection state shows on the Sensors pill with live RPM, so a dropped link looks dropped. Readings are recorded but deliberately not feeding the score yet — they get validated against a real car first.
✓
Your garage follows the account, not the phone Aug 16
Signing in brought your drives and left your cars behind, because the garage only ever existed in localStorage on the phone that created it. It now syncs to the account on sign-in, on launch, and on every edit. Garages merge rather than overwrite — either direction of overwrite silently destroys a vehicle someone typed in by hand — resolving per-vehicle by newest-wins, with exactly one active car guaranteed.
✓
Scores made impossible to assert Aug 16
Groundwork for rewards meaning anything. The leaderboard is now derived on the server from recorded drives instead of a number the client posts, and it excludes simulated drives — which could otherwise produce a flawless run from a desk. It exposes a handle and a score: no user id, no device id, no coordinates. Also closed a hole where any signed-in user could claim all 154 unowned drives in the table, and added vehicle_id to drives so a per-vehicle or per-corridor title can actually be defended.
✓
Sign-in unblocked — the code now fits, and the mail is fast Aug 15
Two independent faults, both fixed. The real one: the project mailed 8-digit codes into a field capped at 6 characters, so the last two digits couldn't be typed — sign-in was impossible, not merely awkward, and no amount of waiting for email would have helped. Codes are now 6 digits, which is Supabase's floor; the field was correct all along. The second: Supabase's shared mailer had delivered a code more than an hour late, past its own 60-minute expiry. Mail now leaves through Resend on the verified smoothafdriving.com. The expiry was deliberately left alone — a code that expires before arriving is a latency problem, and widening the window would only enlarge the guessing surface.
