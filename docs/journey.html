The route

chronological — the order a real driver hits them
1

Discovery

"A friend beat my drive — what is this?"

TodayWord of mouth and a manual TestFlight invite. smoothafdriving.com is a marketing/brand site, and /tracker still serves a dead, stale tracker from the old codebase.

  • High
    No App Store presence — installing means getting a TestFlight invite and owning the TestFlight app. That's two unfamiliar steps before the app even opens.
    Publish a public TestFlight link now; App Store submission as the real unlock. One tap, no invite chase.
  • Med
    The /tracker page looks broken — it's what made the app seem dead before. A curious visitor lands on a corpse.
    Turn /tracker into a "Download the app" page. Kill the confusion, capture the intent.
  • Med
    No built-in way to share a score — the most natural growth loop (brag → friend installs) isn't wired to the front door.
    Every shared score card carries an install link straight back to this stage. Close the loop.
2

First open

"Okay, I'm in. Now what do I do?"

TodayLands straight on the home screen — "Hey, Driver." No value intro, no "mount your phone," no permission priming. The first-run onboarding is designed but not built.

  • High
    A cold drop onto home, with nothing framing what the app is for or what to do next. The only intro (a name + email prompt) doesn't appear until they try to drive.
    Build the first-run intro: three quick value cards → "mount your phone" → permission priming, before the first drive. Set the expectation, then earn it.
3

Identity

"Do I have to sign up before I can even try it?"

TodayAnonymous use works — you can drive without an account, and drives get claimed to you the moment you sign in. Sign in with Apple is one tap; email is a 6-digit code.

  • Low
    Good bones — no signup wall. But the value of signing in (your history follows you to any phone) is never sold, so most people won't.
    Lead with Apple. Offer sign-in after the first great recap — "keep this drive forever" at peak delight, not as a gate up front.
4

Add your car

"Which of these is my car? Do I need a photo?"

TodayA garage with eight vehicle types and an optional car photo. The prompt to add a car fires as the first drive starts.

  • Med
    The car prompt interrupts the exact moment they're trying to pull away and drive — the worst time to ask them to browse vehicle types.
    Move the car pick into the calm first-run, not the drive moment. Make the garage a reward, not a toll booth.
  • Med
    Car photos live only on the device — sign in on a new phone and your cars are there but their photos are gone.
    Sync car photos to the account alongside the garage, so identity is truly portable.
5

First-drive setup

"I tapped record… why is it just sitting there?"

Today"Acquiring GPS…", then location + motion permission prompts (iOS needs a tap for motion). Then a 10-second calibration while moving. If a permission is denied, the drive aborts with a toast.

  • High
    Permissions are asked cold, at the worst moment, with no "why." Deny one and the drive dies on a toast — a dead end, not a recovery.
    Prime permissions in first-run with the reason. On denial, show an inline "Enable location" button, not a toast that strands them.
  • Med
    The 10-second calibration needs the car moving — a first-timer sitting in a parked car can read the wait as "stuck / broken."
    Say it plainly: "Drive normally for 10 seconds while we calibrate." Turn dead air into a clear instruction.
  • Low
    Location only runs in the foreground, so handing a destination to Maps would freeze recording. Background location isn't shipped yet.
    Add Always-location so recording survives a Maps handoff — unlocks the navigation flow.
6

The drive

"Oh — the edges glow when I brake too hard. I get it."

TodayThe strong part. A big live speed and score, screen edges glowing green → amber → red for how you're driving, an optional OBD sensor drawer, screen kept awake. Glanceable at speed, no reading required.

  • Low
    Mostly solid. The Sensors drawer used to black out the drive and the OBD readout jittered as revs changed — both just fixed.
    Hold the line: keep it glanceable. Resist adding anything that needs reading while moving.
7

The recap

"91. And it actually described how I drove."

TodayThe payoff, and it lands: a score, seven dimensions, a map, and a coaching line that reads the shape of the trip — all on one screen. This is the peak emotional moment of the whole journey.

  • High
    This peak is where two loops should close — capture (sign in to keep it) and growth (share the score) — but neither is front-and-centre here.
    Put "Save this forever" and "Share your score" right on the recap. Convert delight into an account and a referral in the same breath.
8

Coming back

"Am I actually getting smoother on my commute?"

TodayDrives group by road corridor, and there's a server-derived leaderboard. The Trends screen's data is built and tested — the tap-through UI is the missing piece. Goals aren't built yet.

  • Med
    The core retention hook — "am I improving?" — isn't visible yet. Trends data exists but has no screen, and the leaderboard shows empty "Corridors / Rivals" tabs.
    Ship the Trends UI; fill or hide the empty leaderboard tabs so nothing reads as broken.
  • Low
    Cross-device history works, but a resold or new phone won't pull back the detailed GPS map tracks — only the summaries.
    Fine for now; revisit if testers ask "where's my route from last week?"
