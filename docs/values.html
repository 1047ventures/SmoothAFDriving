Smooth AF · values & vision · Oct 2026

Smooth is the skill.

Smooth AF is a driving app that scores how smoothly you drive and coaches you to drive better. It reads your phone's motion sensors, GPS and, when you plug one in, your car's own computer. This page is what we believe, how we decide, and where you can see those beliefs in the code.

What it is

A score you'll chase, and a teacher underneath it

US roads are rough because most people learned to drive at sixteen and never revisited it. Aggressive, reactive driving is the norm. We think the fix is drivers who read the whole road: the light two blocks ahead, the brake lights three cars up, the merge before it happens. That driver is smooth, efficient and calm, and they make everyone around them calmer too.

The hook

A number, points, a leaderboard

Every drive gets a score out of 100. Every smooth mile banks points. Beat the ETA while staying smooth and you earn a bonus. It's a game because games get played.

The point

You, measurably better

Improvement over time is the North Star. The app tells you whether you're getting smoother, what habit is holding you back, and what to try next drive. The score is how we keep you coming back long enough to change.

How we decide

The order we decide in

When two good things pull against each other, this is the tie-breaker. The ranking is deliberate, including the third one.

1st

Make a better driver

Every feature answers to this. A cool car feature that doesn't change how someone drives waits. A plain one that does ships first. That is why "are you getting better?" shipped before navigation, and why Car Health explains codes instead of just listing them.

2nd

Tell the truth

A driver changes their habits based on what we show them, so a wrong number does real harm. We show a blank before a guess, and "we couldn't read it" before a false all-clear. If the data can't support a claim, the app doesn't make it.

3rd

Safety third

We don't sell fear, and we don't pitch ourselves as a safety or insurance product. Smooth, systems-thinking drivers are safer drivers, so safety is the result of the first two, and we let it be the result. In the product it is never negotiable: nothing pops up, beeps or asks a question while the car is moving.

The one-question test

"Does this make someone a better driver?"

We ask it of every feature, screen and number. When the answer is "not really, but it's cool," the feature goes in the queue behind one that does.

Principles

What we believe, and where it shows

Each principle is tied to a real decision in the app. If a principle can't point to one, it isn't a principle yet.

Smooth before fast

Speed is easy. Holding a car calm at speed is the skill, so that's what we reward, and fast-but-rough earns nothing extra.

  • AlgorithmBeating the ETA only adds to your score if the drive scored 80 or higher before the clock. Below that, the clock can't lift a rough drive.
  • AlgorithmA speed bonus of up to +5 rewards staying smooth at highway pace, scaled by how smooth you were. Sloppy-and-fast gets almost none.
  • UXThe on-time bonus on the Rewards screen is labelled as a bonus on top of smooth miles, never instead of them.

The road's fault isn't yours

You can't will a red light green. We score what you control and discount what you don't.

  • AlgorithmOnce the recap has looked up nearby signals, a stop at a light, its queue or a stop sign counts a quarter of a stop toward momentum. A stop with no signal nearby counts in full.
  • AlgorithmParked stretches over 3 minutes (gas, coffee) pause the ETA clock. Moving time is what counts.
  • UXThe recap explains stops by cause, so a light-heavy commute reads as the road, not as you.

Points for miles, not minutes

A clean hour on the highway is worth more than a clean minute in a parking lot. Points measure distance driven well.

  • Algorithm10 points per smooth mile. Road within 8 seconds of a harsh moment doesn't count as smooth.
  • UXRewards shows smooth miles, the share of your miles that were smooth, and badges at 10, 50, 100, 250, 500 and 1,000 smooth miles.
  • UXWe removed vouchers that didn't exist. A promised reward we can't deliver breaks the second principle.

A blank beats a guess

When the data can't support a claim, we say less, never more.

  • AlgorithmA drive with no ride-quality data is capped at 95. We can't certify a calm cabin we never sensed.
  • Algorithm"Are you getting better?" only compares drives scored the same way, and skips anything that changed because the app changed.
  • DataA trouble-code read that times out keeps the last good report. It is never shown as "No trouble codes."
  • DataA VIN read that doesn't agree with itself is thrown away rather than matched to the wrong car.

Eyes on the road

The app is used at 70 mph. Anything that competes with the road loses.

  • UXLive feedback lives at the screen's edges as color you read with peripheral vision: green smooth, amber firm, red harsh.
  • UXTrouble codes and "Is this your car?" wait on Home until you've parked. Nothing interrupts a drive.
  • UXNo permission prompts mid-drive. Motion and location go through native iOS services that ask once.

Subtract before you add

Every element on a driving screen costs attention. The default answer to "should this be on screen?" is no.

  • UXThe improvement verdict replaced a line on Home instead of adding a card.
  • UXDriver modes will remove things, never add them. A pro never sees "check your mirrors." A first-timer never sees peak g.
  • UXFake leaderboard tabs with placeholder rows were cut entirely rather than kept as decoration.

Your drives are yours

We collect where you drove and how. That data belongs to the driver.

  • DataDrives are stored on your phone first. The cloud copy exists so your history follows you to a new phone.
  • DataCloud drive rows are readable only by their owner. GPS tracks were locked down after we found they were readable by anyone.
  • DataThe leaderboard is built on the server from your saved drives, not from a separate score the app sends.
  • DataYour VIN goes to NHTSA's public decoder and nowhere else. Deleting a drive deletes its cloud copy.

Verify on the real thing

Code that reads well has still shipped broken. We check what actually happens, then say so plainly.

  • BuildEvery change gets an independent reviewer whose job is to find how it fails, and every screen change is rendered and critiqued at phone size.
  • BuildThe pedal and stop anchors are fitted to real drives; the ride-composure anchors are still provisional. When the engine changes, stored drives are rescored so history stays comparable.
  • BuildWhen something hasn't been tested on a real car yet, the roadmap says so.

Layer 1 · the score

Out of 100

The weighted sum, plus a speed bonus for staying smooth at pace. A perfect 100 is meant to be nearly impossible.

score = Σ dim × weight
+ up to 5 (avg 25→65 mph) × base/100
cap 95 without ride data
Layer 2 · the clock

Destination drives

Set a destination and arrive. Beating the ETA can lift the score by up to 15, only when the drive scored 80+ before the clock. Arriving late costs points only when the ETA was traffic-aware.

bonus = 15 × beat/20% × base/100
if base ≥ 80, else 0
Layer 3 · points

Smooth miles

Points bank every drive, destination or not. The on-time bonus stacks on top under the same smoothness gate.

points = 10 × smooth miles
+ up to 50% if on time & base ≥ 80

And the verdict on you

"Are you getting better?" compares your last 5 drives with the 10 before them: hard brakes, launches and turns per 10 miles (pooled by distance, so a short hop can't swamp a long drive), the share of miles driven smooth, pedal smoothness and score. A change has to clear 20% on a rate or 3 points on a score to count. It leads with progress, and only names a slip when nothing improved.

The driving fingerprint

A score says how smooth. A fingerprint says how you drive.

Two drivers can post the same score through the same bend. The difference shows up in the accelerator pedal traced through the corner, against the g-force the car actually pulled. That pairing is the richest signal we can read, and it's where the score is heading.

Brake & gun

In too hot, then a stab

Full lift, hard brake into the apex, then slam the throttle out. The g-arc spikes late and lopsided.

Carry the line

Steady in, smooth out

Ease off, carry speed to the apex, roll the throttle back on. One clean, symmetric hump.

From the dongle

  • Throttle and pedal in the score
  • True wheel speed, RPM, load, gear recorded
  • Torque → horsepower, temps, fuel level, voltage recorded
  • VIN and trouble codes read on connect

From the phone

  • Forward/back g and jerk in the score
  • Cabin vibration (ride composure) in the score
  • GPS route, so the same road is compared against itself live
  • Sideways g through each corner next

Not on a standard dongle

  • Brake pedal and steering angle live on the car's private network. We rebuild both from what we can see: pedal at zero + speed falling + backward g is a brake, graded by its shape.
  • We say so up front rather than promise data we can't read.

Where it's going

Your car's concierge, and a coach in the passenger seat

Maps apps have grown bloated and Waze rewards you for reporting, not for how you drive. There is room for one honest, driver-first app that covers a driver's whole life with their car.

Shipped

Score, points, improvement, car health

Smooth-mile points, "are you getting better?", plain-English trouble codes, and the car identifying itself when you plug in.

Next

Coaching that remembers

Tips that notice a pattern ("third drive braking late"), suggest a drill, and back off once you've fixed it.

Later

Modes, from never-driven to pro

Same engine, different voice. First Timer gets a pre-drive walkthrough and gentle reminders to scan; Teen links to a parent; Pro gets every number and the leaderboard. Modes remove things, never add them.

Later

The concierge

Your car's manual in plain English, what each warning light means, maintenance timelines, and a proactive heads-up before a code becomes a breakdown. The free scan stays free; the ongoing watch is the paid tier.

Horizon

Human coaches

When a habit won't budge, a real coach reviews telemetry no driving instructor has ever had: every harsh moment on a map, the pedal through every corner. Async review, then ride-alongs, then programs. Teens and their parents first.

What we still owe

Where we fall short today

The second principle applies to this page too.

Calibration

The scoring anchors are fitted to a few drivers' real drives. With more drivers they will move, and a city-heavy driver may still read a little low.

Untested

Car Health and the VIN read are tested against the reply formats dongles use, not yet on a real car.

Security

Three pre-launch database functions trust a bare device or account ID: two can read a driver's full drives and one can delete them. They must be removed or locked down before public launch.

Integrity

The app computes each drive's score on the phone and uploads it, so a determined user could post a score they didn't earn. Scoring on the server from the uploaded track would close that.

Braking

The braking score was meant to reward longer approaches too, but the approach length currently comes out the same for almost every stop, so only how gently you slowed counts.

Portability

On a new phone, points for older drives are estimated from the score, because the cloud doesn't store them yet.

Rewards

Points can't be redeemed for anything yet. Until they can, the app says so.
